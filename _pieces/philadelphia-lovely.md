title: "Philadelphia, Lovely"
date: 2026-03-23

subtitle: "The true tale of my very first day spent in the City of Brotherly Love."
---

I sat in the Barnes and Noble Starbucks cafe on the second floor of the University of Pennsylvania bookstore and watched a man disengage from his close reading of a John Wick graphic novel in order to focus his full attention on the patron taking a seat in front of him. The man’s new friend was old, with deep wrinkles and wisps of gray hair long enough to tickle his shoulders. His wrinkles deepened further as he began delineating the virtues of Dr. Jordan B. Peterson and the “horrific lifestyle” of homosexuals.

“I had a friend in middle school who was gay,” offered the younger man.

The old man wiped his nose and sniffed.

“Well, me too,” he said. “But they’re not all like that.”

The younger man seemed to accept this. The older continued his monologue, and the younger provided him with appropriately timed grunts and nods, so as to indicate his rapt attention regardless of how often his eyes flicked back to the action-packed pages of his comic book.

The three of us sat like that for a while. To the older man’s credit, his musings from then on were mercifully less hateful. Superstitious, yes. Conspiratorial, for sure (his father was on the Eldridge when they wormholed down to Norfolk!). But he didn’t seem to have his heart in the real, hardline bigotry.

Soon after, the John Wick fan took advantage of a lull to wow his new friend. Philadelphia, he said, was the only American city mentioned in the Bible. In the book of Revelation, John the Baptist spent several verses explaining God’s plan to turn Philadelphia into the new Kingdom of Zion. Philadelphia was, most likely, also the legitimate location of the original Garden of Eden.

To both my and the younger man’s dismay, the elder did not seem much interested in this sort of talk. His energy was primarily devoted to identifying any breath taken or pause allowed by the younger so that he could interject once more. This dynamic frustrated me, and I decided it was time to move on.

My girlfriend was touring the Weitzman School of Design, to which she had recently been accepted on a substantial scholarship. I was on my own for the day. My first stop had been the aforementioned Barnes and Noble Starbucks cafe on the second floor of the University of Pennsylvania bookstore because I was entertaining fantasies of reading books, studying up on my supposed area of interest, and generally spending time investing in the betterment of myself.

Witnessing what I had in the cafe had shot that all to hell. I blamed the location and decided that I would try another coffee shop, one more tied into the local culture and residents. I pictured myself, brows furrowed, absorbed in my book, surrounded by young, attractive professionals doing the same.

It was after sitting down on the westbound T1 trolley that I overheard my second conversation of the day.

A: “I find that hard to believe.”

B: “There was a little telekinesis at work. I’m telling you.”

A: “Moving stuff with your mind? I just find that a little hard to believe.”

B: “Understand, man. Elon Musk, those people, they wanna misalign our vibrations. That takes away our power.”

A: “For sure. But, if you think about it, Satan is the real enemy. He’s the devil.”

B: “Man, I’d kick Satan down the stairs.”

A: “Ha! Not if he kicks you first.”

B: “No. I’d wait for him to turn his back. When he wasn’t looking, I’d kick him down the stairs. He won’t even know it was me. See, when he looked back, I’d turn around like I was trying to find the guy that did it. But really it was me the whole time.”

C: “In Canada, a guy went to jail for doing graffiti on a Satanic monument.”

A: “Pfft.”

B: “Canada.”

To put it plainly, the subsequent time I spent peering out the window of my authentic Philadelphia coffee shop was basically a waste. I finished an audiobook that was about basketball, but also about life and death and the American dream. I had a croissant sandwich. It left the corners of my mouth feeling greasy and unclean (prior experience had taught me that there was no real remedy to this sort of sensation). It was around three at this point, and I discovered that Smokey Joe’s happy hour began at four, so onto another trolley I hopped.

Inside Smokey Joe’s, with my forearms subtly sticking to the laminated wooden bar top, I witnessed a conversation between two very old men. They were clearly regulars. Retirees, probably, that spent the weeks between their grandkid’s visits in the old bar. One wore a timeless Flyers cap, and the other wore an Eagles 2025 Superbowl Champions hoodie. I will refer to them as Gritty and Swoop.

“When I moved in 25 years ago,” said Gritty, “everything was fine. I checked, and I wasn’t in a flood zone. Now, I get a letter from my insurance telling me that my house is at risk, and they’re raising my rates out the wazoo.”

“I’m a weather buff,” said Swoop.

“Mhm.”

“So I know some stuff. Lotta people don’t know this, but Philadelphia gets more rain than Seattle!”

Swoop let this fact sink in for a moment.

“In 2013, we got eight inches in a single day. You remember that one?”

“Sure I do!” responded Gritty. He sounded defensive. “That was Floyd. And my house didn’t flood.”

“Hell no, it wasn’t Floyd! 2013 broke Floyd’s record. Floyd was in ‘99, and it only dropped six and a half or so.”

“You’re the weather buff.”

Swoop knew the other three biggest rainfalls in Philadelphia history. He also knew the five biggest snowfalls in Philadelphia history. Gritty was unimpressed. He began gazing into his \$8 pitcher and only looked up when Swoop began listing off the same information for the city of Boston, Massachusetts.

“Boston’s a hellhole. My daughter moved up there. Don’t know what she’s thinking.”

“Whatever man,” said Swoop. “The weather’s the weather.”

Gritty started in on Boston again, but he was interrupted by a young woman sitting a few seats over.

“How about those Bruins?” she offered with a cheeky smile.

This was not well received by the old men. It seemed to make them uncomfortable. Gritty offered a curt nod and sort of shrug of his lips. Swoop stared straight ahead.

“Without McAvoy, I think they have to write off the whole season.”

Swoop’s gaze fell on the woman now. He nodded.

“I’d say so,” he said.

“Me too,” said Gritty. “I hated that Four Nations shit anyway.”

Not long after that, I reunited with my girlfriend. I was glad to hear that she had a good day and had made a couple of friends. This was a day trip only, and we had a train to catch at six. We crossed Walnut and Chestnut at a brisk pace. It was raining. It had been raining all day.

As we crossed Market Street, we were approached by a short man in a gray hoodie. He was stubbled and smelled like cigarettes. He clutched a beautiful bouquet of red roses in one hand.

“Is this your first time here?”

The question was directed at my girlfriend.

“Yes!” I responded warmly. “We thought it was lovely.”

“Well, I want to give you one of these.”

He plucked a rose from his bouquet and offered it to my girlfriend. She accepted with a giggle.

“Come on, man,” I said.

I didn’t mean it like that. I meant to express delight, tinged with disbelief. Reflecting, as I am now, I understand why he took it the way he did.

“No worries, brother. I got one for you too!”

I plucked the rose from his hand and smiled. The short man told us that he was headed to see his mom for dinner. That explained the roses. I wondered if he was going to lock us into one of those meandering, one-sided conversations wherein I repeat the same Huhs, Reallys, and Wows for fifteen minutes. I have very little ability to extricate myself from these types of conversations, and I knew that my girlfriend was well aware of this deficiency.

These fears consumed me for a moment. I barely registered the man telling us not to miss our train and wishing us well on our way home. He walked away. I was left with a rose in my hand and the foul taste of my own cynicism in my mouth.
