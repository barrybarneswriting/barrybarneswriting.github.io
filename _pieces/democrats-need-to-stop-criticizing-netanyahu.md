In May 2026, following the perennial fight over the mandatory conscription of ultra-orthodox Jews by the Israeli military, the Israeli parliament, called the Knesset, voted for its own dissolution. This move will most likely mean nationwide elections by October and the potential end of Benjamin Netanyahu’s time as Israel’s Prime Minister and, perhaps, his time as a free man at all. It won’t be the ICC that gets him. War criminals are so rarely arrested for war crimes. Good old-fashioned corruption will probably be his undoing.

But, here’s the thing I keep coming back to. Netanyahu is so visible, so vile, and so easy to point to as the bad thing. He’s Israel’s Trump or Hitler or, occasionally, Stalin! Now, finally, many Democrats have started pointing this out. It is true that he is vile, a war criminal, and even a disastrous leader for the state of Israel, but this focus on Netanyahu is incomplete at best and nefarious at worst.

Mikie Sherrill, the recently elected governor of New Jersey has described her views on the matter quite succinctly: “October 7 was horrific, the hostages need to be released, Israel has a right to exist, Netanyahu has been a really bad actor in this space”. Progressive Democratic Senator Brian Schatz said that “Netanyahu is making Israel and Israelis and Jews unsafe all over the world”. Bernie Sanders, who now concedes that Israel has committed a genocide in Gaza, still seems intent on focusing his criticism on “Netanyahu’s right-wing, extremist, and racist government”. Cory Booker has talked about his “disagreements with the actions of the Netanyahu government”. Chris Murphy has bemoaned Netanyahu’s actions as a setback for U.S.-Israeli relations and believes we “shouldn’t concede that \[.\..\] if you don’t support what Netanyahu is doing right now inside Gaza then you don’t support Israel”, the implication, of course, being that the optimal position is one of disagreement with Netanyahu and support for the state of Israel. I’m not going to waste your time with more statements from people like Chuck Schumer and Nancy Pelosi, but you can use your imagination.

Democrats consistently identify Netanyahu as some aberrational Israeli evil. They would have you believe that he is particularly far-right and particularly bloodthirsty. Don’t get it twisted; he is both far-right and bloodthirsty. However, identifying him as a uniquely extremist politician would be a bit like calling Ted Cruz a fringe Republican figure. Ted Cruz is right-wing, certainly, but he is nothing special in American politics. Hell, Cruz might be going too far. Calling Netanyahu an extremist might be more like calling Chuck Schumer a left-wing radical. Let me explain.

Netanyahu is the leader of the Likud party. Heading into the dissolution of the Knesset, Likud led the governing coalition, thus making Netanyahu the Prime Minister of Israel. The election in October will, potentially, bring in a new majority party and/or a new Prime Minister. There are a few likely candidates: the recently formed Together party, a joint slate led by former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett and opposition leader Yair Lapid, is currently expected to win around 18 seats and was leading in the polls until recently. Itamar Ben-Gvir and the Jewish Power Party are expected to win eight or nine seats. Bezalel Smotrich, who has traditionally run alongside Ben-Gvir, is running with the Religious Zionist Party and is polling around five seats. Gadi Eisenkot is running with Yashar, and is the current frontrunner for Prime Minister. Yashar is expected to win 20-22 seats.

One could probably guess the beliefs of people leading parties called the Jewish Power Party and the Religious Zionist Party. To summarize, Ben-Gvir and Smotrich are some of the most monstrous, violent politicians in the world. They proudly call Palestinians names like subhuman, dogs, and vermin. They openly take great joy from the genocide in Gaza. Evil, evil men, these two.

Okay, of course these guys were going to be psychos, but who are the moderates? Well, Bennett and Eisenkot, of the Together party and Yashar party respectively, are both to the “left” of Netanyahu. That is not saying much. Bennett, the more hardline of the two, basically invented the notion of mowing the lawn. (Palestinians are the lawn, the mower is Israeli missiles, and the chore must be conducted routinely so as to keep the grass from becoming overgrown). He has bragged about killing Arabs, denied any distinction between Palestinian children and terrorists, and stated that he would do “everything in \[his\] power, forever, to fight against a Palestinian state”.

Lucky for us, Bennett’s favorability in the polls has fallen as of late. The favorite, as it currently stands, is Gadi Eisenkot. He is known as the most moderate among these favorites. After a long career in the Israeli military, Eisenkot was nominated to serve as its Chief of Staff by Benjamin Netanyahu, a position he held from 2015-2019. He is legitimately more moderate than the other options, but, again, that’s not saying much. Eisenkot historically supported a two-state solution, though his views have shifted significantly since October 7, 2023. He has stated that anyone advocating for a Palestinian state does not understand the depths of the conflict. He has expressed anger towards Netanyahu for bowing to ceasefire demands from the United States. He has insisted on retaining the Jordan Valley and illegal settlements in the West Bank. This is the moderate candidate.

Let’s say October rolls around, Netanyahu is voted out, and we get the best case scenario. Netanyahu would be replaced by a Prime Minister (and governing coalition) that does not believe in a Palestinian state, has railed against ceasefire after ceasefire, and rejects the return of illegal landgrabs to the Palestinian people. This will serve as pressure release valve, especially for those politicians in the United States that are facing mounting scrutiny over Israel’s genocide and their funding of it. These politicians, who have cleverly focused their criticisms on Netanyahu rather than the fascist system that created him, will celebrate a return to sanity in the land of Israel. They will congratulate the people of Israel for having the courage to oust the man singularly responsible for the, um, oversights in Gaza. We can expect that they will continue to enable the violent colonial project in both rhetoric and armaments.

We must stop criticizing Netanyahu’s government because Netanyahu’s government is not the problem. This is part and parcel with the politicians who seem incapable of recalling any events in the history of the region prior to October 7th, 2023. The same ideology that gave rise to Netanyahu is to blame for the violence of October 7th and the countless inhumanities suffered by Palestinians since 1948.

Zionism, defined as the belief in or support for a Jewish state of Israel, is inherently violent, racist, and exclusionary. Does Israel have the right to exist as a Jewish state? Of course not! Does the United States have the “right” to exist as a white nation or as a Christian nation? Does it have the right to enforce such a vision through terror and destruction? Israel, since its inception, has been a place where someone born on the other side of the world can have more rights as a citizen than someone born on the land as a simple function of their ethnicity, race, or religion. Zionism necessitates a Jewish state, which necessitates a Jewish majority, which necessitates some means by which non-Jewish people are deprived of rights, representation, equal citizenship, and their home.

This is not to free Benjamin Netanyahu or the countless other complicit Israeli politicians, military members, and citizens from blame. Rather, it is to say that Zionism is the foundational ideology of Israel. That is the problem. It cannot be fixed by voting in some other flavor of monster to spearhead some other flavor of atrocity.

When Netanyahu leaves office, and he will, one way or another, I believe Democrats will miss the moment, purposefully or otherwise. Cory Booker will post a vertical video celebrating the end of the extremist Netanyahu government. Television liberals will support Naftali Bennett or Gadi Eisenkot through a press tour, proclaiming that a new day has dawned in Israel. It will be critical that activists do not allow the ongoing suffering of Palestinians to be swept under the rug along with Netanyahu’s familiar face. They must continue to name Zionism as a violent, racist ideology and, critically, explain exactly why that is.

The public consciousness has moved dramatically since October 7, 2023. It will feel good to see the face of such horrors leave government. It will feel even better if he ends up in prison (don’t get your hopes up). But we cannot allow his eventual departure to be a pressure release valve. This is not the “back to brunch” moment. The pressure must stay. It must continue to build. Keep boycotting, keep punishing electeds who fail to meet the moment, keep providing aid and visibility in whatever way you can. The next surge of support for Palestine cannot arrive after this new flavor of monster and their new flavor of atrocity arrives. It has to be ongoing. It has to.
